Relocating

Your job offer is the loan.
Close like it could change.

17 November 2026 By Kenny Pleasant 7 min read

Rates came down again last week. Freddie Mac put the 30 year fixed at 7.11% on 12 November, down from 7.18% the week before. A year ago it was 6.24%, so nobody should be throwing a party, but it is moving the right way and I can feel buyers waking back up.

A lot of the people I talk to right now are relocating. They have an offer from a company here, or a transfer, and they want to buy rather than throw money at rent. I get it. But there is a conversation I have with every one of them that I almost never hear other agents have, and in a year like this one it matters more than the rate.

When you move here for a job, the job is what the bank is lending against. Not your old salary, not your savings, not your credit score on its own. The job. And the lender checks that the job still exists right before you close.

What the job market actually looks like

You have probably seen the headlines about tech layoffs in Seattle. Some of it is real and some of it is noise, so here are the numbers.

Washington's Employment Security Department reported that the state lost an estimated 15,400 jobs in September, seasonally adjusted. Statewide unemployment was 4.8%, down from 4.9% in August and up from 4.7% a year earlier. Over the full year the state is still up 9,200 jobs, which is growth of 0.3%. That is not a boom. It is barely growth.

Here is the part that cuts against the headlines. In that same September report, the information sector, which is where a lot of tech sits, added 1,400 jobs. Professional and business services added 1,800. Most of the September loss was in the public sector.

Then on 2 November Amazon filed a notice with the state covering 95 layoffs in Washington, in Seattle, Bellevue, Redmond, Sumner and Pasco plus remote staff, with separation dates from 7 December through 25 January. Amazon said these were part of cuts it had already announced, not a new round.

So my honest read is this. It is not a collapse. It is a drip. Small rounds, steady, at companies people move here for. That is exactly the kind of environment where a buyer feels safe, signs, and then gets a calendar invite they were not expecting three weeks before closing.

The check most buyers never hear about

Under Fannie Mae's guidelines, which most conventional loans follow, the lender has to verify your employment no more than 10 business days before the note date. The note date is basically your closing. Usually that verification is a phone call or a third party database check to confirm you still work there. If the lender cannot get that verification, Fannie Mae will not buy the loan, and a lender is not going to fund a loan it cannot sell.

Think about what that means in practice. Your approval letter was issued weeks ago. You did the inspection, negotiated the repairs, maybe already scheduled the movers. None of that matters if your employment cannot be confirmed in those last two weeks. If you lose the job in that window, you almost certainly lose the loan.

Whether you also lose your earnest money depends on how your contract was written and where you are in the timeline. That is a conversation to have before you sign, not after.

The lender does not care how good the job sounded in the interview. They care whether it is still there the week you sign.

Buying before your first day

A lot of relocation buyers want to close before they start, so they can move once. That can be done. Fannie Mae has a specific set of rules for qualifying on an offer letter, and they are stricter than most people expect.

If you are closing before you have a paystub from the new job, the loan has to be a purchase of a one unit home you will live in. Your start date has to fall no earlier than 30 days before closing and no later than 90 days after it. The lender needs a fully executed, non contingent offer or contract. And on top of the normal reserves, the lender has to document either six months of the full house payment in reserves, or enough money to cover all your monthly debts from closing until your start date, plus one more month.

Two things in there catch people. First, non contingent. If your offer letter still has open conditions, like a background check or a start date that says "to be determined," ask the lender early how they will treat it. Second, the reserves. Six months of payment on a Seattle house is real money that has to be sitting in your account after your down payment and closing costs.

What that looks like on a King County house

The NWMLS October report put the King County single family median at $925,000, down 7.2% from a year ago. Active listings in King County are up 32.8%. Across the whole NWMLS area there are 4.41 months of inventory, up from 3.02 a year ago.

I ran that median through today's rate. These are my numbers, not a lender quote. Assumptions: $925,000 price, 20% down, a $740,000 loan, 30 year fixed at 7.11%, principal and interest only. That comes to about $4,978 a month. Six months of that is about $29,868, and the real reserve number will be higher once property taxes, insurance and any HOA dues are added in, because the rule is based on the full payment.

So a relocation buyer closing on an offer letter at the median needs the down payment, the closing costs, and roughly another $30,000 or more sitting in the bank. Most people I talk to have not planned for that last piece.

The good news is in the same report. More listings and more months of supply means you do not have to rush. A year ago a relocation buyer felt pressure to buy in the first month or lose out. Today you have room to wait until you have a couple of paystubs, and the house will very likely still be there, or one just like it.

The case for renting first

I sell real estate. I am still going to tell you that some relocation buyers should rent for the first six to twelve months.

If your new team or your new company has already been cutting, if your role is new and not yet proven, or if your whole plan only works on one income that is less than a year old, buying in your first ninety days puts a lot of weight on something you cannot control. In a market where the King County median is down 4.1% from a year ago, being forced to sell a house you bought last year usually means bringing money to the closing table, not walking away with any.

Renting first also lets you learn the area. Plenty of people pick a neighborhood off a map, then find out what the commute actually feels like in February. I wrote about the real cost of moving here in an earlier piece on relocating to Seattle in 2026, and that math still holds.

When buying right away makes sense

There are plenty of relocation buyers for whom buying now is the right call, and I would tell them so.

Two incomes where either one carries the house. If one of you could make the payment alone, a layoff is painful but survivable.

Real reserves after closing. Not the six months the lender requires. More than that. If you could cover the payment for a year without a paycheck, you have time to find the next job without selling.

A transfer, not a new hire. Moving within a company you have been with for years is a different risk from starting fresh with a team you have never met.

A home you would keep through a bad year. Buy the house you would be fine living in if the next two years are rough, not the one that only works if everything goes right. Same logic I laid out in the adjustable rate breakdown: pick the version you can live with in the bad case.

What I would actually do

  • Before you write an offer, ask your lender exactly which employment documents they need and when they will verify again. Get the answer in writing.
  • If you are closing before your start date, confirm your offer letter is fully executed and has no open conditions. If it has any, get the lender's position on it before you shop.
  • Budget reserves on the full payment, including taxes and insurance, not just principal and interest. On a median King County house plan for well over $30,000.
  • Read your financing contingency with your agent and know exactly what happens to your earnest money if the loan fails because of employment.
  • Ask HR whether your relocation package has a repayment clause if you leave or are let go early. Know that number before you buy.
  • If your role is new and your company has been cutting, seriously consider renting six to twelve months first. The inventory is not going anywhere.
  • Use the extra listings. In this market you can negotiate on price, repairs and closing timing. A longer close that lands after your first paystub is a fair thing to ask for.

If you are relocating to King, Snohomish or Pierce County and trying to figure out whether to buy now or rent first, I am happy to walk through your actual situation with you. That conversation is worth having before you sign anything.
